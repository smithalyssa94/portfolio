The Problem

Sensata Technologies is a $4B global sensing company with 20,000 employees, products in everything from aircraft to electric vehicles, and a brand identity stuck in 2006.

The business transformed under new leadership, with restructured business units, and a stock that had rallied over 100% in a couple quarters. The brand hadn't kept up.

The Rundown

  • 37+ stakeholder interviews
  • Cross-industry competitor audit
  • Audience perception analysis
  • Brand framework
  • Brand architecture
  • Messaging toolkit
  • Website / digital presence
  • Hype video
  • Internal rollout collateral

The Thinking

The brand needed to encompass where the company was growing without losing what was already true. At the top of the framework, the positioning had to be honest to who Sensata actually was — not aspirational language layered over a company that wouldn't recognize itself. Then underneath, the messaging house had to allow for specificity across wildly different customer types without breaking the brand apart.

The Work

Brand Framework

Six rounds of iteration to get to a north star, purpose, identity, four key messages, and three industry narratives. The framework had to hold for automotive and aerospace and defense and industrials — segments that have almost nothing in common except the underlying technology. Every round sharpened the distinction between what was aspirational and what was earned. The CEO approved it in a 30-minute session that ended five minutes early.

Messaging

10+ customer-specific toolkits, each built from the same template: headline, USP, supporting messages, proof points, key message tags, where-to-use guidance. The point wasn't to write different messages for every audience — it was to give every audience a version of the same message that felt like it was written for them. A product marketer in Japan and a sales engineer in Germany should be able to pull from the same system and still sound like they're talking about their customer's specific problem.
