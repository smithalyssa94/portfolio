The Problem

Stakeholder and internal interviews are where the real insights live — and they're brutal to manage at scale.

The traditional trade-off: take detailed notes and lose presence in the room, or stay present and lose the granular language that matters when you're writing the messaging architecture in six weeks. With AI as my notetaker, I'm able to stay present in the conversation and build a searchable repository of business context for later.

The Rundown

  • Full-lifecycle interview workflow
  • Claude Desktop + Granola + Obsidian
  • Pre-interview research reads
  • Thesis-blind question generation
  • Structured post-call processing
  • Four-lens synthesis framework

The Thinking

The interviews are the most important part of a brand engagement — the language people reach for, the stories they tell unprompted, the moment someone says the thing that changes the whole framework. I didn't want to miss that because I was writing notes. AI handles the admin so I can stay in the conversation, and the structured processing means nothing gets lost between the interview and the deliverable.

The Work

Pre-Interview

Within the skill, Claude organizes all interviewees and does some early research on the industry, organization, and the person I'm interviewing, then generates questions based on that information. Claude reads everything already in the vault — competitive audits, prior interview notes, strategic briefs — and generates questions grounded in what's known. The point is to go in sharp, not open-ended-for-the-sake-of-it. And the whole system is thesis-blind: if the working thesis uses a phrase, that phrase never comes out of the interviewer's mouth. Every question is a listening device, not a leading one.

In-Call

Granola captures the transcript, which means my notes in the room are freed up for the things a transcript can't capture — energy shifts, body language, the moment someone leans in or pulls back, the thing they almost said and didn't. That's where the real signal is, and you lose it if you're heads-down writing.

Post-Call Processing

I establish the themes/ideas to look for when setting up the project, and after each interview Claude reads the Granola transcript then documents and organizes things like impactful quotes, proof points, divergence signals, etc. This is documented in Obsidian so I can search and reference later as well. The template forces rigor — every interview gets the same treatment whether it's interview three or interview thirty-three.

The Result

The system runs on Claude Desktop with Filesystem and Granola MCP integrations. The skill architecture, question generation logic, and processing templates are original work.
